# The visitor who arrives with only a sign

**Fictional decision packet and sample review.** The documents below illustrate a choice at a sculpture park. No device, sign or visitor was tested for this example.

**Decision:** Should the park manager adopt proposal B, replacing the map rack at the unstaffed entrance with a QR sign? The alternative is to keep printed maps. The manager's condition is that a first-time visitor with a compatible phone but no mobile reception can get the full map at the entrance, unaided and without preparing at home.

| Packet item | What the reviewer has to work with |
|---|---|
| A · Sign proof | “Scan to open the trail map.” The code points to the map's web address. The sign contains no map. |
| B · Walk note | The walker's note describes opening the map on office Wi-Fi, turning off mobile data at the entrance and following the already-open map along the trail. |
| C · Proposal B | The author cites the walk as the reason the rack can be removed. No record describes first opening the map at the sign. |

**Review note for the manager**

> **Revise proposal B: keep the printed maps.** The walk note supports keeping the web map as an option for visitors who load it before setting out. It does not support making the sign the only way to get a map. The walker arrived with the map; the visitor in the decision criterion arrives with its address.
>
> **Confidence:** High that the walk did not test first opening. Whether first opening would succeed is unknown. The sign proof and walk note do not establish whether visitor Wi-Fi is available and discoverable at the entrance. Nothing in this review establishes that scanning the sign fails.
>
> **Smallest check that could change the advice:** Ask a first-time user at the sign to open the full map on a compatible phone that has never loaded it or joined the park network. Turn mobile data off; leave ordinary visitor Wi-Fi available if it exists. Give no staff directions. Record whether the user can get from the sign to the usable map. Another walk is unnecessary.
>
> A successful opening would support considering removal for the conditions actually checked, not a claim about every phone or every visitor. If the user cannot open it, retain an on-site map option under the current criterion. The manager decides whether the evidence is sufficient or whether to change the service offered. Saving print costs would be a reason to reconsider that service, not evidence that the sign delivers it.

**Check on the hand-off:** A establishes what the visitor starts with; B establishes what the walker started with; C makes the inference under review. The proposed first-opening check has no result in the packet. The decision stays with the manager.
