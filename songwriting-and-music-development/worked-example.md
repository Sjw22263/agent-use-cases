# Worked example: a lyric edit and a specific sound change

**Illustrative text exercise.** The song fragment and sound direction below were invented to demonstrate the collaboration. They are not drawn from an actual song or session. No audio was generated or auditioned.

## What the songwriter brings

The songwriter supplies this verse and refrain and wants the verse to feel conversational. The third line can change; the refrain stays as written.

```text
[Verse 1]
The rain lets up at half past six
I open up the kitchen door
I can already hear the magpies in the yard
The day can start without me now

[Refrain]
Leave the kettle on a while
Nothing needs an answer yet
```

The songwriter also gives a specific Style revision: replace the acoustic guitar’s full strums with fingerpicking in the verse. Keep the easy four-beat pulse, close lead vocal and light brushed snare. Keep soft full strums in the refrain.

## What the agent offers

- **Lyric:** The stretch from al-**READ**-y to **HEAR** to **MAG**-pies gives the line more to fit into the verse’s pulse. That run of words can convey anticipation, but it may also bunch up. The agent offers “I **hear** the **mag**pies in the **yard**”. The shorter line gives the stressed words more room without having to lean on “in the”. It keeps the sound and setting but loses the emphasis of “already”. If that anticipation matters, the songwriter can keep the longer line and try it against the melody instead.
- **Style:** The agent writes the guitar instruction as a contrast between sections. “Fingerpicked guitar” on its own would leave the refrain change unstated. There is no reason to rewrite the vocal or drum direction for this request.

## Copy-ready option for review

The Lyrics block shows the shorter line so the songwriter can try it in context. It is a proposal, not an approved replacement.

**Title:** Leave the Kettle On

**Styles**

```text
Unhurried acoustic folk in a steady four. Close, conversational lead vocal and light brushed snare throughout. Fingerpicked acoustic guitar in the verse; soft full strums in the refrain, with the pulse unchanged.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Verse 1]
The rain lets up at half past six
I open up the kitchen door
I hear the magpies in the yard
The day can start without me now

[Refrain]
Leave the kettle on a while
Nothing needs an answer yet
```

## What the human checks

1. **Choose the lyric.** Speak both third lines into the fourth without pausing to reset the beat. Does the shorter line sit more naturally, or does the longer one say something worth the extra time? The songwriter decides which to carry forward.
2. **Check the guitar change.** If the operator wants to compare takes, use the same chosen lyric in both versions and change only the guitar cue. Listen at the verse-to-refrain transition: can the individual picked notes be heard in the verse, do soft strums return in the refrain, and does the voice stay close over the same pulse?

The agent supplies the text and listening questions. The operator generates, listens and decides whether to keep a take. There is no take or listening result in this example.
